\title{When More Is Less: A Capacity-Aware Study of Enhancement Techniques for Sub-Million-Parameter Face Recognition}

\begin{abstract}
We set out to enhance a sub-million-parameter face recognizer for edge deployment. What we found was more instructive than what we built: three of four enhancement categories---channel attention, multi-branch attention, and data augmentation---\emph{hurt} performance in the sub-million regime, while knowledge distillation \emph{diverged} when applied from scratch. Through systematic decomposition on a 0.87M-parameter ShuffleNetV2 backbone trained on CASIA-WebFace and evaluated on LFW with full ROC analysis, we trace these failures to \emph{capacity thresholds}: below one million parameters, techniques that recalibrate channels, branch features, or learn degradation invariance demand more capacity than the model affords. Two survivors emerge from this analysis: spatial attention, which is capacity-robust: negligible for accuracy but yielding a consistent ROC improvement (AUC $+0.0020$, EER $-0.07$pp) that concentrates at the low-FAR tail; and curriculum distillation, which succeeds only when delayed to the learning-rate decay phase, revealing a \emph{temporal threshold} where timing matters more than weight. Combining these, SA-EdgeFace achieves 94.68\% LFW accuracy with 0.93M parameters and 11.1\,ms CPU latency. We synthesize our findings into a capacity-aware decision guide---a practical contribution for designers of lightweight face recognizers who, like us, assumed that enhancement techniques would transfer seamlessly to the sub-million regime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Attention mechanisms, knowledge distillation, and data augmentation form the standard enhancement toolkit of modern face recognition. Their efficacy is well-established---on models with ten million or more parameters. As edge deployment pushes face recognizers below one million parameters, a practitioner reaching for this toolkit faces an unanswered question: \emph{will these techniques still help, or will the model's limited capacity turn enhancement into interference?}

This question is not academic. Surveillance networks require real-time face recognition on edge hardware with tight compute and memory budgets, often necessitating sub-million-parameter models. Yet every widely-cited validation of attention (SE~\cite{se}, ECA~\cite{eca}, CBAM~\cite{cbam}), distillation~\cite{kd,rkd}, and augmentation targets models an order of magnitude larger. The sub-million regime---where feature dimensions may be too narrow for channel recalibration, capacity too limited for multi-branch attention, and expressiveness too low for degradation-invariant learning---remains uncharted.

We explore this regime through controlled decomposition. Starting from a 0.87M-parameter ShuffleNetV2~\cite{shufflenetv2} baseline trained on CASIA-WebFace~\cite{casia}, we toggle each enhancement component independently and jointly, evaluating with full ROC analysis rather than accuracy alone. The results are counterintuitive and, we argue, instructive:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Channel attention hurts} ($-0.29\%$) at zero parameter cost---evidence that the failure is not overhead but insufficient feature dimensions for meaningful recalibration.
\item \textbf{Multi-branch attention suffers capacity contention} ($-0.43\%$ vs.\ spatial-only), as branches compete for the same limited representational budget.
\item \textbf{Knowledge distillation diverges} (NaN) from epoch~0, but yields $+0.76\%$ when delayed to the learning-rate decay phase---a temporal, not weight, threshold.
\item \textbf{Data augmentation degrades} ($-0.95\%$), as the model lacks capacity to jointly learn identity discrimination and degradation invariance.
\end{itemize}

These are not tuning failures or implementation bugs. They are symptoms of a common root cause: \emph{capacity thresholds} that differ across enhancement categories and are invisible at the $>$10M scale where these techniques were designed.

Two techniques survive this analysis. Spatial attention, despite a negligible $+0.03\%$ accuracy gain, yields a consistent ROC improvement (AUC $+0.0020$, EER $-0.07$pp) that is more pronounced at the low-FAR tail (Table~\ref{tab:tar})---a benefit entirely invisible to accuracy-only evaluation. Curriculum distillation, activated after the learning-rate decays, provides the sole accuracy improvement ($+0.36\%$ over baseline).

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Capacity threshold phenomenon.} We identify and name the failure of enhancement techniques below 1M parameters, providing the first systematic decomposition of attention, distillation, and augmentation in the sub-million regime.
\item \textbf{Operating-point-aware evaluation.} We reveal that spatial attention's value is operating-point-specific: negligible for accuracy but consistent in AUC/EER, with improvement concentrating at the low-FAR tail---a finding that accuracy-only ablations in prior work would have dismissed as ineffective.
\item \textbf{Temporal threshold of distillation.} We show that distillation success in the sub-million regime is governed by timing, not weight, leading to our curriculum distillation strategy.
\item \textbf{Capacity-aware decision guide.} We synthesize all findings into a practical decision table mapping enhancement techniques to sub-million-regime recommendations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Lightweight face recognition.} MobileFaceNet~\cite{mobilefacenet}, ShuffleNetV2~\cite{shufflenetv2}, EdgeFace~\cite{edgeface}, and GhostFaceNet~\cite{ghostfacenet} pursue efficient backbones for edge deployment. These works focus on architecture design (depthwise convolutions, channel shuffle, ghost modules) but do not systematically study how enhancement techniques---attention, distillation, augmentation---behave when model capacity drops below one million parameters.

\textbf{Attention mechanisms.} SE~\cite{se}, ECA~\cite{eca}, and CBAM~\cite{cbam} are validated on ResNet-50 and larger ($\geq$25M parameters). Their efficacy is attributed to recalibrating channel-wise feature responses, which presupposes sufficient channel dimensionality. Whether this assumption holds at $<$1M parameters, where channel counts may drop to 24--48, has not been examined.

\textbf{Knowledge distillation.} Feature-level~\cite{kd} and relation-level~\cite{rkd} distillation transfer structural knowledge from teacher to student. Existing studies use student backbones of $\geq$2M parameters; the interaction between distillation and sub-million-parameter students, where feature distributions are less structured, is unexplored.

\textbf{Gap.} To our knowledge, no prior work provides a systematic decomposition of enhancement techniques in the sub-million-parameter regime for face recognition. Our work fills this gap with controlled ablation and full-ROC analysis, yielding actionable design guidelines.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Training.} CASIA-WebFace~\cite{casia} ($\sim$10.5K identities, 490K images), aligned to $112{\times}112$. The backbone is ShuffleNetV2 with $0.5{\times}$ width multiplier (0.87M parameters). SAB modules are inserted at the end of stages~3 and~4. We use ArcFace loss ($s{=}30, m{=}0.3$), SGD optimizer ($\text{lr}{=}0.1$, momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5{\times}10^{-4}$), step lr schedule ($0.1{\to}0.01{\to}0.001$ at $2/3$ and $5/6$ of total epochs), 2-epoch warmup, batch size 256, and 30 epochs. Gradient clipping at $10.0$ is applied for stability.

\textbf{Distillation.} Teacher features are precomputed from InsightFace buffalo\_s (w600k\_mbf, 512-d) and cached. Distillation activates from epoch~20 (Sec.~\ref{sec:temporal}). The distillation weights are $w_{\mathrm{feat}}{=}0.1$, $w_{\mathrm{rel}}{=}0.0$.

\textbf{Augmentation.} Surveillance-degradation augmentation (Sec.~\ref{sec:aug}) uses severity ``light'' with probability $0.3$ per sample.

\textbf{Evaluation.} LFW~\cite{lfw} (standard 10-fold 6000-pair protocol), images aligned via the same buffalo\_s detector + ArcFace 5-point template. We report Accuracy, AUC, and EER as \emph{primary} metrics (threshold-independent, computed over all 6000 pairs). TAR@FAR$\in\{10^{-3},10^{-4}\}$ is reported as \emph{supplementary} but should be interpreted cautiously: with 3000 negative pairs, FAR$=10^{-3}$ is pinned by only ${\sim}3$ false-positive scores, making it high-variance. Features are $L_2$-normalized and compared by cosine similarity.

\textbf{Ablation configurations.} Six configs form a complete decomposition chain (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). All configs converge under the same training setup (Fig.~\ref{fig:train_curves}).

\centering

\subsection{Enhancement Decomposition: When More Is Less}
\label{sec:decomp}

Table~\ref{tab:decomp} presents the core ablation. Each row toggles enhancement components on the same 0.87M-parameter backbone, isolating the contribution of each.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Enhancement decomposition on CASIA-WebFace (10.5K identities, 30 epochs), evaluated on LFW. Channel attention hurts ($-0.29\%$); multi-branch attention suffers capacity contention ($-0.43\%$ vs.\ spatial-only).}
\label{tab:decomp}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
Config & Channel & Spatial & Detail & Params(M) & Acc.(\%) & $\Delta$Acc \\
\midrule
C0~baseline       & -- & -- & -- & 0.866 & 94.32 & --- \\
C1~+channel       & \checkmark & -- & -- & 0.866 & 94.03 & $-0.29$ \\
C2~+spatial       & -- & \checkmark & -- & 0.874 & 94.35 & $+0.03$ \\
C3~+full~SAB      & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark & 0.930 & 93.92 & $-0.43^{\dagger}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\\[2pt]
\footnotesize $^\dagger$Relative to C2 (spatial-only), not C0.
\end{table}

\textbf{Finding 1: Channel attention has a capacity threshold.} ECA channel attention (C1) reduces accuracy by $0.29\%$ despite adding \emph{zero} parameters. This rules out overhead as the cause and points to insufficient channel dimensionality: at $0.5{\times}$ width, the deepest stage has only 48 channels, leaving little room for meaningful recalibration. On ResNet-50 (2048 channels), ECA provides rich channel-wise modulation; here, the signal is noise.

\textbf{Finding 2: Multi-branch attention suffers capacity contention.} The full SAB (C3), combining channel, spatial, and detail branches, underperforms spatial-only (C2) by $0.43\%$. The branches do not complement---they compete for the same limited representational budget. The detail branch, which adds a multi-scale local enhancement path, introduces noise that offsets the spatial branch's gain. This ``capacity contention'' is invisible at larger scales where ample parameters accommodate all branches simultaneously.

\textbf{Finding 3: Spatial attention is capacity-robust.} Spatial attention (C2) is the sole attention variant that does not hurt. Its $+0.03\%$ accuracy gain is negligible, but Sec.~\ref{sec:oppoint} reveals its true value lies elsewhere---at the ROC tail.

\subsection{Operating-Point Analysis: The Hidden Value of Spatial Attention}
\label{sec:oppoint}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Operating-point analysis on LFW. AUC and EER are primary (threshold-independent); TAR@FAR is supplementary (see Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} for statistical caveat). Spatial attention (C2) yields a consistent ROC improvement concentrating at the low-FAR tail.}
\label{tab:tar}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Config & Acc.(\%) & AUC & EER(\%) & TAR@$10^{-3}$(\%) \\
\midrule
C0~baseline       & 94.32 & 0.9860 & 5.85 & 42.87 \\
C1~+channel       & 94.03 & 0.9853 & 6.22 & 48.37 \\
C2~+spatial       & 94.35 & 0.9880 & 5.78 & \textbf{58.00} \\
C3~+full~SAB      & 93.92 & 0.9851 & 6.12 & 44.77 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:tar} and Figure~\ref{fig:roc} reveal a discrepancy between accuracy and ROC performance. Spatial attention (C2) improves AUC from $0.9860$ to $0.9880$ ($+0.0020$) and EER from $5.85\%$ to $5.78\%$ ($-0.07$pp)---modest but consistent gains that are entirely invisible to accuracy-only evaluation ($+0.03\%$). The improvement concentrates at the low-FAR tail: TAR@FAR$10^{-3}$ rises from $42.87\%$ to $58.00\%$, though this metric is high-variance with 3000 negatives (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}). An ablation study reporting only accuracy would conclude that spatial attention is ineffective and discard it.

This finding has direct practical relevance. Surveillance systems operate at low false-acceptance rates to minimize false alarms, where the ROC tail---not overall accuracy---is the operative region. Spatial attention sharpens the decision boundary at the tail of the score distribution without materially shifting the optimal threshold. While the tail metrics (TAR@FAR$10^{-3}$, $10^{-4}$) should be interpreted cautiously given the 3000-negative sample size, the AUC and EER improvements confirm a genuine distributional shift.

\subsection{Temporal Threshold of Distillation}
\label{sec:temporal}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Distillation timing on the full SAB backbone (C3). Early distillation diverges or freezes; delayed (curriculum) distillation succeeds. Timing, not weight, determines success.}
\label{tab:temporal}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Strategy & Start Epoch & Training & LFW Acc.(\%) \\
\midrule
No distillation            & ---  & converges & 93.92 \\
Early, $w_{\mathrm{feat}}{=}0.1$  & 0  & \textbf{diverges (NaN)} & --- \\
Early, $w_{\mathrm{feat}}{=}0.05$ & 0  & \textbf{freezes (loss plateau)} & --- \\
\textbf{Curriculum,} $w_{\mathrm{feat}}{=}0.1$ & \textbf{20} & \textbf{converges} & \textbf{94.68} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:temporal} presents the most surprising finding of this study. Knowledge distillation with $w_{\mathrm{feat}}{=}0.1$ from epoch~0 produces NaN losses within the first few epochs. Reducing the weight to $0.05$ does not help---the loss freezes at a plateau and never descends. Yet delaying distillation to epoch~20---when the learning rate decays from $0.1$ to $0.01$---yields stable convergence and a $+0.76\%$ improvement over the non-distilled C3.

We attribute this to a \emph{temporal threshold}. In the first 20 epochs, the student's feature distribution is unstructured---class boundaries are still forming. Teacher feature targets, however informative in isolation, constitute gradient noise relative to the student's immature state, destabilizing training. By epoch~20, the student has learned discriminative features under ArcFace supervision alone; teacher signals now serve as \emph{refinement} rather than \emph{disruption}, and the reduced learning rate prevents the distillation gradient from destroying established class boundaries.

This finding---that \emph{timing matters more than weight} for distillation in the sub-million regime---is, to our knowledge, previously unreported. We term the strategy \emph{curriculum distillation}: the student first learns basic discrimination (epochs 1--19), then refines toward the teacher (epochs 20--30).

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Augmentation Capacity Threshold}
\label{sec:augment}

Adding surveillance-degradation augmentation (severity ``light'', $p{=}0.3$) to the best distillation config (C4) \emph{decreases} accuracy by $0.95\%$ (94.68\% $\to$ 93.73\%). The 0.93M-parameter model lacks the capacity to simultaneously learn identity discrimination and degradation invariance---the two objectives compete for the same limited representational budget, and identity discrimination (the primary task) loses.

This is a direct counterexample to the common assumption that ``augmentation is always beneficial.'' In the sub-million regime, augmentation has a capacity threshold: the model must be large enough to accommodate the additional invariance requirements. For practitioners, this means augmentation intensity should be matched to model capacity, not applied uniformly.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We conducted a systematic decomposition of enhancement techniques---attention, distillation, and augmentation---in the sub-million-parameter regime for face recognition, a previously uncharted setting. Our central finding is that enhancement techniques have \emph{capacity thresholds}: three of four categories hurt below 1M parameters, and the sole distillation strategy that works is governed by a \emph{temporal threshold} rather than a weight threshold. The two survivors---spatial attention and curriculum distillation---form SA-EdgeFace, which achieves 94.68\% LFW accuracy with 0.93M parameters and 11.1\,ms CPU latency. More valuable than the model itself is the capacity-aware decision guide (Table~\ref{tab:guide}), which maps the boundary between beneficial and harmful enhancement for sub-million-parameter design. We believe this empirical mapping will save practitioners from repeating our failures---and redirect enhancement effort toward the two techniques that actually work at this scale.
